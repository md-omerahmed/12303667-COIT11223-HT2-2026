# Artefact 1: Scholarly Article
Artificial Intelligence – Ethical Decision Making is a logic programming approach.
<img width="2844" height="1342" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/72eacb08-c398-4cae-a4ac-d25449e9320b" />
https://doi.org/10.3390/ai5040130

# Summary of the Artefact
Machado et al. (2024, p. 2710) provide a description of how utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, and ethical pluralism can serve as guidelines for AI decision making. Utilitarianism emphasizes the results, Kantian deontology emphasizes duties and moral rules, irrespective of results. The authors propose a logic-programming approach that combines strict and flexible ethical rules, allowing AI to deal with situations where different ethical principles conflict (Machado et al. 2024, p. 2714).

# Justification on why I chose the artefact
This article was selected because it directly relates to Kantianism and utilitarianism from Week 4. Earlier I thought that AI could just be built around a single ethical theory, but after reading the article, I can now see that that might not be enough. It made ethics seem relevant to what I might have to make as an ICT professional.

# Artefact 2: Blog Article
The 21st Century Trolley Problem — Ethics and Dilemmas of Self-Driving Cars
<img width="2840" height="1330" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/eae56e17-8e36-4221-8bdc-c6d0343b67f5" />
https://medium.com/@mariamzhorzholiani/the-21st-century-trolley-problem-ethics-and-dilemmas-of-self-driving-cars-e77a69e8c21a

# Summary of the Artefact
Zh (2026) uses utilitarianism, Kantianism and virtue ethics when a self-driving car is presented with several choices that are harmful. Utilitarian reasoning is more focused on reducing overall harm while Kantian reasoning is more focused on the question of whether or not one intentionally harms someone.

# Justification on why I chose the artefact
I selected this artefact because the answers are very different depending on the ethical framework that is used when arriving at a response to the same situation. It contributed to an appreciation that ethical theories are more than just academic; they can affect the manner in which engineers design autonomous systems and decide how technology reacts to actual risks.

Using AI to accuse students of cheating at the University.

# Summary of the Artefact
Landymore (2025) describes instances at Australian Catholic University where students have been charged with violations due to AI use in Turnitin, though some charges were later retracted.

# Justification on why I chose the artefact
I selected this, as it inverts our workshop's GenAI situation. But, from a Kantian point of view, if a student is punished based on unreliable evidence, then the student may be treated as a means to uphold academic integrity and not as an end in itself. This also challenges the concepts of fairness, accountability, and trust in automated decision making.

# Summary of the Artefact
The “App Release Dilemma” at Workshop 4 had a profound impact on my thinking about ethical considerations. Looking back on this, I would have been releasing buggy software first come first served to get a head start on the competition. I changed my mind after reading about the possible monetary and trust issues for users.

# Justification on why I chose the artefact
I used Kant's Categorical Imperative to also help me understand that ethics is more than just about consequences (Quinn 2020, pp. 69–70). When I contrasted this with utilitarianism (Quinn 2020, p. 78), I discovered that occasionally, at times, two theories may arrive at the same conclusions… for different reasons. As a future ICT professional, I learned that I have to take into consideration the consequences, duties and the rights of the stakeholders in the important decisions of technology choices.
