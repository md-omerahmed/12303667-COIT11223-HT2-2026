# Summary about the artefact
The updated guide by the Commonwealth Ombudsman clarifies that automation means both rules-based and machine learning. It recognises that these are key considerations, including ‘legality, fairness, rationality, transparency, and privacy', as well as accessibility for vulnerable and non-digital users (Commonwealth Ombudsman 2025, pp. 7–8). The artefact illustrates that there were various factors involved with making an acceptable government decision, one of which is correct computation. A system also has to be within the scope of the agency's decision making authority and responsibility.

# Justification and Reflection
I picked this guide as it gave me a practical basis for which to assess all further artefacts. Using a Kantian perspective, accessible explanations and review respect people as participants with legitimate interests. What I'm reading into this is that the ICT professional should determine if there are circumstances that are relevant and that could be taken into account, but not assume that consistent processing means fair treatment.

# Artefact 2: Independent scrutiny of Australian tax systems
Australian National Audit Office 2025, Governance of artificial intelligence at the Australian Taxation Office.


# Summary about the artefact
The Australian National Audit Office determined the arrangements for the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) to support the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in the organisation were partially effective and recommended that the ATO implement seven recommendations – all of which were accepted by the ATO. According to the audit snapshot, by August 2024, 74% of production AI models did not have completed data ethics assessments (Australian National Audit Office [ANAO] 2025). Especially, the report reports that the 43 models developed by the ATO did not make a complete automatic decision but rather assisted in the decision making process.

# Justification and Reflection
The audit is the one I picked as exam because it is an independent piece of work that looks to evidence to test organisational commitments. Ethical consideration for decision support is warranted even as it may affect whose situation is studied. This is related to the aspect of professional competence and accountability. My key learning is that I have learned to distinguish between missing assurance evidence and demonstrated discriminatory outcomes. The audit does not result in the identification of a bias in every model, it only identifies governance weaknesses.
